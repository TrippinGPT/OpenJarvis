# Trippin Labs SlapDesk Overview

## Purpose

Trippin Labs SlapDesk is the AI-assisted music workflow lane inside the broader Trippin ecosystem.

It is not meant to replace producers, artists, or engineers.

It is meant to make a session more legible:

- what stage the record is in
- what needs attention next
- what should be cleaned up before handoff
- what the producer or engineer should listen for

## What SlapDesk is

SlapDesk is a structured workflow concept for:

- FL Studio cookups
- arrangement and groove checks
- low-end discipline
- mix-prep handoff
- Pro Tools mix-session readiness
- bounce sanity checks

In plain terms: it is a music-session operating layer, not just a vibe or a name.

## Who it is for

SlapDesk is for:

- producers building ideas quickly in FL Studio
- artists who need a cleaner session path from cookup to bounce
- engineers receiving stems, notes, and mix prep
- small teams that want a repeatable handoff language instead of memory and guesswork

## What problem it solves

Music workflows break down when:

- ideas get built fast but not organized
- the groove is interesting but not intentional
- low end gets sloppy
- stems are exported inconsistently
- the engineer receives a session with weak notes
- the final bounce is not checked against the intended feel

SlapDesk solves the workflow problem around the record:

- what stage it is in
- what to check next
- how to hand it off cleanly

## What SlapDesk is not

SlapDesk is not:

- a full autonomous producer
- a replacement for taste
- a replacement for real engineering judgment
- a live FL Studio controller
- a live Pro Tools controller
- a plugin management platform
- an audio-capture system
- an automatic mastering pipeline

Right now it is a structured product/workflow lane, not a DAW automation product.

## Where SlapDesk fits in the ecosystem

- **Relay** is the strongest active product lane and the clearest current shell.
- **OpenClaw / SignalForge** remains the backbone engine and reporting/intel layer.
- **SlapDesk** sits under the Trippin Labs umbrella as the music workflow lane.
- **Discord / public shell** should present SlapDesk as a real lane, but not the current active priority.

The clean mental model is:

- Relay = cockpit
- OpenClaw / SignalForge = engine and intel backbone
- SlapDesk = music workflow lane

## Workflow shape

SlapDesk works best as a session path, not just a feature list.

### 1. Cookup

Idea and build phase.

Purpose:

- get the main idea down
- define the record identity early
- capture the hook, bounce, tone, and direction

Questions it answers:

- what is the beat trying to be
- what is the core energy
- what should not be lost later

### 2. Pocket

Groove and arrangement feel phase.

Purpose:

- tighten rhythm feel
- check bounce, swing, space, and movement
- make sure the record actually sits right instead of just sounding busy

Questions it answers:

- does the groove breathe
- is the arrangement helping or crowding the record
- where is the bounce actually coming from

### 3. Low End

Bass, kick, and sub discipline phase.

Purpose:

- make low-end choices intentional
- reduce mud and collision
- decide what owns the bottom at the right moments

Questions it answers:

- who owns the low end
- where are the kick and bass stepping on each other
- is the bottom controlled or just loud

### 4. Slap Map

Session game-plan phase.

Purpose:

- define the record roadmap
- mark what matters most
- decide what needs to stay forward and what can stay supporting

Questions it answers:

- what are the hero elements
- what is the intended emotional path
- what does the mix need to preserve

### 5. Trunk Check

Energy and translation check.

Purpose:

- test whether the record still hits outside the producer bubble
- evaluate impact, movement, and replay energy

Questions it answers:

- does it still knock
- does it translate beyond studio headphones
- is the energy real or imagined

### 6. Stem Run

Export and organization handoff phase.

Purpose:

- clean up exports
- name and group stems clearly
- make the next handoff less painful

Questions it answers:

- are stems organized
- are exports consistent
- can another person open this without guessing

### 7. Mix Sheet

Mix-prep notes and engineer handoff.

Purpose:

- summarize intent
- capture what to protect
- document what to fix, feature, or mute

Questions it answers:

- what should the engineer know first
- what matters most in the record
- what should not get buried or over-processed

### 8. Bounce Check

Final output sanity pass.

Purpose:

- check the final bounce against intent
- catch obvious misses
- confirm the record still feels like the original target

Questions it answers:

- did the final output preserve the slap
- did something important disappear
- is this ready to send, post, or archive

## Recommended first-use flow

For a simple first-use path:

1. Cookup
2. Pocket
3. Low End
4. Slap Map
5. Trunk Check
6. Stem Run
7. Mix Sheet
8. Bounce Check

This does not need to be rigid.

The point is to give the record a real workflow spine instead of treating every session like a random pile of files and opinions.

## Module summaries

- **Cookup** — define the idea fast
- **Pocket** — tighten groove and movement
- **Low End** — discipline bass, kick, and sub
- **Slap Map** — define the session game plan
- **Trunk Check** — test impact and translation
- **Stem Run** — organize exports for handoff
- **Mix Sheet** — give the engineer a clean brief
- **Bounce Check** — verify the final output still hits

## What a session looks like with SlapDesk

A session with SlapDesk should feel like this:

- the producer knows what stage the record is in
- the arrangement and low end get intentional attention
- the session has a clear map before handoff
- the engineer gets organized stems and useful notes
- the final bounce gets checked against the original target, not just exported and forgotten

That is the lane.

Not automation theater. Not magic. Clean workflow.
