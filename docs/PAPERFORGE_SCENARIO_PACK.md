# PaperForge Scenario Pack

## Purpose

This is the first reusable scenario pack for PaperForge, the first active fake-money operating environment inside PaperLab.

It turns the workflow packet into practical simulation drills that can be repeated, logged, reviewed, and refined.

This pack is strictly for paper practice.

- fake money only
- no real execution
- no wallet connection
- no private keys
- no swaps
- no broker or exchange routing
- no guarantees
- no financial advice

Use this pack with:

- [PAPERLAB_OVERVIEW.md](PAPERLAB_OVERVIEW.md)
- [PAPERFORGE_WORKFLOW_PACKET.md](PAPERFORGE_WORKFLOW_PACKET.md)

## How to use this pack

Each scenario is a drill, not a prediction.

The objective is to improve:

- observation quality
- setup labeling discipline
- fake entry and invalidation clarity
- logging honesty
- review quality
- refinement quality

If the setup is unclear, overhyped, structurally messy, or impossible to define cleanly, skip it.

## Starter order

For a beginner, use the first pack in this order:

1. Range Rotation
2. Trend Pullback
3. Breakout Continuation
4. Rejection / Failed Move
5. Momentum Chase Trap
6. News / Hype Spike Caution

That sequence keeps the learner focused on cleaner structure first and messier, more emotionally loaded setups later.

## Do not simulate this

Skip the scenario if any of the following are true:

- you cannot describe the market condition in one sentence
- the setup label keeps changing while you stare at it
- invalidation is vague or emotional
- the fake entry idea exists only because price is moving fast
- the setup depends on hype, hope, or hindsight
- you are trying to prove you were right instead of running a clean drill

## Process quality rubric

Use this instead of fake P&L as the first score.

- `0` = no clean observation, no usable log
- `1` = partial setup, weak labeling, weak review
- `2` = clear setup and log, but weak invalidation or lesson
- `3` = clear setup, clean fake plan, honest review, one usable refinement

This is a discipline score, not a performance promise.

## Scenario 1: Breakout Continuation

### Setup description

A level breaks with visible participation and the market shows signs that the move may continue instead of immediately failing.

### What to observe

- whether the level was meaningful before the break
- whether the break is clean or sloppy
- whether momentum expands or immediately stalls
- whether the market condition supports continuation or only a short burst

### What to label

- continuation candidate
- clean break versus weak break
- confidence level
- surrounding market condition

### Fake entry idea

Simulate only if the break holds or reclaims cleanly after the level is crossed.

### Invalidation idea

The break fails quickly, loses acceptance, or returns back through the level without clean continuation behavior.

### Fake target or management idea

Use the next obvious structural area as the first management reference rather than assuming unlimited expansion.

### Not worth simulating if

- the break comes from a meaningless level
- the move is already extended before the fake entry idea is defined
- there is no clean invalidation
- the market is obviously choppy and rejecting follow-through

### Common discipline mistakes

- chasing late because the candle looks exciting
- labeling every fast move a breakout
- ignoring that the level was weak to begin with
- moving invalidation farther away after the fake entry idea is written

### Review questions afterward

- was the level actually worth caring about
- did continuation show up or was it only a one-candle burst
- did the fake entry idea rely on confirmation or pure excitement
- was the invalidation realistic

## Scenario 2: Rejection / Failed Move

### Setup description

Price pushes into a level or direction, fails to hold, and shows evidence that the attempted move was rejected rather than accepted.

### What to observe

- whether the rejection happens at a meaningful area
- whether the market snaps back quickly or drifts weakly
- whether the failed move has context behind it
- whether follow-through appears after the rejection

### What to label

- failed break
- rejection setup
- confidence level
- context quality

### Fake entry idea

Simulate only if the failed move is visible enough that the rejection has actual structure, not just one random reversal candle.

### Invalidation idea

The market reclaims the rejected area and starts accepting above or below it again.

### Fake target or management idea

Use the midpoint of the prior move or the opposite side of the local structure as the first management reference.

### Not worth simulating if

- the rejection area is not meaningful
- price only flicked the level once with no follow-through
- the reversal case exists only in hindsight
- invalidation would be based on emotion rather than structure

### Common discipline mistakes

- calling every pause a rejection
- assuming reversal without evidence of failure
- forcing a dramatic narrative onto a minor wobble
- grading the setup by outcome instead of read quality

### Review questions afterward

- was the failure actually visible in real time
- did the rejection happen at a level that mattered
- did the response move justify the label
- did the fake plan respect structure or just chase reversal hope

## Scenario 3: Range Rotation

### Setup description

Price is rotating inside a recognizable range and reacting between areas instead of trending cleanly.

### What to observe

- whether the range is real and repeated
- whether support and resistance are obvious
- whether the range is stable or breaking down into noise
- whether the midpoint matters

### What to label

- range condition
- edge reaction versus midpoint chop
- confidence level
- volatility quality

### Fake entry idea

Simulate only near a meaningful range edge or after a clear reaction that gives the range thesis a real structural basis.

### Invalidation idea

Range support or resistance fails cleanly and the market starts accepting outside the structure.

### Fake target or management idea

Use the midpoint as the first management reference and the opposite side of the range as the fuller target reference only if the session context supports it.

### Not worth simulating if

- the range exists only because the chart is zoomed too tightly
- the range edges are vague
- the middle of the range is being treated as a setup zone
- the market is transitioning into something cleaner or messier and the range label is stale

### Common discipline mistakes

- forcing trades in the middle of the range
- pretending a messy chop box is a clean range
- aiming for full-range rotation when only partial movement is realistic
- refusing to update the label when the range breaks

### Review questions afterward

- was the range actually stable enough to matter
- did the fake entry idea respect edge versus midpoint discipline
- was the target logic realistic for the session
- did the setup stay a range or quietly become a different condition

## Scenario 4: Momentum Chase Trap

### Setup description

A fast move looks attractive precisely because it is already moving, but the simulated drill is about recognizing when that urge is low quality.

### What to observe

- whether the move is extended
- whether the context supported the move before it accelerated
- whether the move is happening into a problem area
- whether the urge to act is coming from structure or adrenaline

### What to label

- chase risk
- extended move
- low-quality momentum
- emotional pressure level

### Fake entry idea

If simulated at all, the point is usually to document why the chase is weak, not to glorify late action.

### Invalidation idea

If there is no clean invalidation, that alone is the warning sign.

### Fake target or management idea

If the learner insists on documenting the scenario, keep the management idea conservative and focused on demonstrating why late momentum entries are hard to manage cleanly.

### Not worth simulating if

- the setup exists only because price is moving fast
- there is no prior plan or structural reason
- invalidation cannot be stated clearly
- the learner is trying to turn FOMO into a method

### Common discipline mistakes

- calling adrenaline conviction
- pretending a late move has the same quality as an early setup
- widening invalidation because the entry was already poor
- reviewing the setup as good if fake P&L happened to work

### Review questions afterward

- did the idea come from structure or urgency
- was the setup late by definition
- did the scenario prove anything useful besides chase risk
- what earlier clue would have been better than reacting late

## Scenario 5: Trend Pullback

### Setup description

A broader directional move is intact and the drill is about whether a pullback offers a cleaner simulated continuation entry than the original impulse move.

### What to observe

- whether the trend is actually intact
- whether the pullback is controlled or chaotic
- whether the pullback respects useful structure
- whether the trend is already tired

### What to label

- trend condition
- pullback quality
- continuation potential
- confidence level

### Fake entry idea

Simulate only if the pullback is controlled enough that continuation logic still makes sense.

### Invalidation idea

The pullback breaks the structure that made the trend label valid, or starts showing acceptance against the original directional thesis.

### Fake target or management idea

Use the prior impulse structure or the next obvious level as the first management reference rather than assuming trend expansion forever.

### Not worth simulating if

- the trend label is stale
- the pullback is actually a larger structural failure
- the market is rolling into chop
- the learner cannot explain why the trend still deserves that name

### Common discipline mistakes

- calling every pause a pullback
- confusing late trend exhaustion with continuation
- ignoring how messy the pullback became
- assuming the prior impulse guarantees another one

### Review questions afterward

- was the trend still valid when the drill was labeled
- did the pullback stay controlled enough for continuation logic
- was the fake entry idea patient or reactive
- what sign would have downgraded the setup sooner

## Scenario 6: News / Hype Spike Caution

### Setup description

An event-driven or hype-driven move creates abnormal energy, but the discipline drill is about caution, not excitement.

### What to observe

- whether the move is event-driven, rumor-driven, or socially amplified
- whether price behavior is orderly or chaotic
- whether spreads, slippage assumptions, or emotional distortion would make the setup poor even in simulation
- whether the move can be described without hype language

### What to label

- event spike
- hype distortion
- unstable context
- low clarity or caution state

### Fake entry idea

If simulated, keep the scenario focused on documenting caution and process quality, not hero behavior.

### Invalidation idea

If the setup cannot define a clean invalidation because conditions are unstable, that is part of the reason to skip or downgrade it.

### Fake target or management idea

Use conservative structural references only. If no stable structure exists, mark it as not worth simulating.

### Not worth simulating if

- the setup depends on social hype rather than structure
- volatility is too erratic to describe cleanly
- invalidation is impossible to define
- the learner is trying to gamble on attention rather than run a drill

### Common discipline mistakes

- confusing noise with opportunity
- writing hype-filled logs instead of clean observations
- acting as if event energy automatically creates edge
- reviewing the scenario only by the size of the move

### Review questions afterward

- was this actually a structured scenario or just volatility theater
- did the log stay factual
- would a cleaner day have taught the same lesson with less distortion
- what caution filter should be stronger next time

```text
PAPERFORGE COMPLETED SCENARIO CARD

Scenario:
Range Rotation

Date:
2026-06-26

Session:
US morning

Instrument / Pair:
Example large-cap equity index proxy

Market condition:
Clear intraday range after an early impulse. No clean trend continuation.

Setup label:
Range reaction off lower boundary

Confidence:
Medium

What was observed:
- lower boundary already reacted twice
- midpoint kept stalling price
- no clean breakout follow-through

What was labeled:
- stable enough range for a paper drill
- partial rotation more realistic than full expansion

Fake entry idea:
Simulate only if lower boundary reacts again with visible hold.

Fake invalidation:
Acceptance below the lower range boundary.

Fake management idea:
Midpoint first. Opposite boundary only if momentum stays orderly.

What made it worth simulating:
- clear structure
- clean invalidation
- repeatable label

What would have made it a no-trade:
- lower boundary becomes sloppy
- sudden breakout pressure changes the condition

Common discipline risk:
Trying to force a full-range target when midpoint is the cleaner first objective.

Review notes:
- lower boundary reaction worked
- midpoint target was realistic
- full-range expectation would have been too ambitious

Lesson:
In medium-confidence ranges, midpoint discipline matters more than heroic target bias.
```

## Suggested first-use loop

A practical first pass through this scenario pack:

1. Pick one of the first three scenarios only.
2. Write one scenario card before the move resolves.
3. Run one fake management plan.
4. Review the result later with one lesson.
5. Stop after one or two clean drills instead of turning the session into a fake overtrading contest.
